# Field log — Claude's mic (M-Audio M-Track Duo / Sennheiser shotgun)

Lab notebook for the audio source Claude operates directly on Shiro. Distinct from Aaron's
other two mics. **Gathering phase — no hypothesis declared, nothing sealed.** Everything
here is observation and instrument calibration.

Raw WAVs live here (gitignored, never committed). Segment index: `segments.csv`.

## Rig (calibrated 2026-07-12)

- **Interface:** M-Audio M-Track Duo, USB → Shiro. avfoundation device `USB AUDIO CODEC`.
  Index drifts between sessions — `record-loop.sh` resolves it **by name** every launch.
- **Mic:** Sennheiser shotgun, XLR → INPUT 1, **+48V phantom ON**. On a stand under an
  umbrella, aimed at the feeder alongside an action cam.
- **Signal on channel 1 only** (ch2 is noise floor) → captured as mono.
- **Format:** 48 kHz / 16-bit PCM / mono, rolling 5-minute segments.
- **Levels at set gain:** ambient RMS ≈ −27 dB, peaks ≈ −10 dB. Good headroom.
- **Weather:** steady light rain all morning. Umbrella over the mic.

Open gather. Feeder soundscape, baseline, first day with Claude on the mic.

**Notes as I go:**

  ffmpeg was a child of the harness. **Lesson: the recorder must outlive the session.**
  Rewrote as `scripts/record-loop.sh`, which double-forks into its own session (macOS has

- **09:00** — read the repo's Berkeley paper (Glassman, Domer & Dudley, *Vocal Dimorphism
  in Anna's Hummingbirds*). It hands us the whole instrument. Their Raven Pro detector:
  **8–10 kHz band**, chip duration **0.01–0.1 s**, min separation **0.05 s**, SNR **+11 dB**
  over a 2 s rolling background. And the measurable: **chip interval** (onset→onset) is
  sexually dimorphic — **male 0.32 s ± 0.08, female 0.43 s ± 0.15**, males ~1.4× faster
  (p < 0.001).

  My first detector was scanning **3.5–9 kHz** — too low, right where the rain lives.
  Rebuilt to the paper's window as `scripts/detect-chips.py`.

- **09:02** — pulled a **positive control** from xeno-canto: **XC1077841**, 54 s of Anna's
  *call* recorded at Dungeness Rec Area, Clallam County WA — same PNW population as our
  birds. See `docs/reference-recordings.md`. Detector finds **95 chips**, interval median
  **0.494 s** (SD 0.085), duration median **38 ms**. Ticks land one-to-one on the chips in
  the spectrogram. **The instrument works on a known target.**

- **09:10** — ran it on our own porch audio. It reported **405 chips in 8 minutes.**
  I did not believe it, and the statistics said not to: a real chipping bout is *rhythmic*
  (reference SD = 0.085), but our "chips" had **SD ≈ mean** — the signature of a Poisson
  process, i.e. random impulses. That's rain.

  **The confound: a raindrop is a broadband CLICK.** It has real energy at 8–10 kHz, so it
  sails straight through a bandpass. The paper's team never had to solve this — they had a
  recordist pointing a mic at a bird. We have a mic under an umbrella in the rain.

- **09:20** — found the discriminator. Not loudness, not band — **spectral shape**:

  | | 8–10 kHz vs 1–3 kHz, during the event |
  |---|---|
  | real Anna's chips (n=95) | **+17.8 dB** — genuinely bright up high |
  | our porch events (n=259) | **−14.7 dB** — energy peaks LOW |

  **32.5 dB apart, zero overlap** — not one of 259 porch events reached even the 10th
  percentile of a real chip. Added a **+6 dB band-contrast gate** (threshold placed in the
  empty gap between the two populations, *before* looking at what survived).

  Effect: reference **98 → 95** (keeps 97% of real chips). Porch **405 → 2**.

- **RESULT, honestly: zero confirmed Anna's chips in the first ~8 minutes.**
  The 2 survivors (178.63 s and 180.36 s into the 08:54 segment ≈ **08:57:26 / 08:57:28**)
  are marginal — contrast **+8.0** and **+6.3 dB** vs. **+14 to +21** for real chips — and
  they sit 1.7 s apart, far too wide to be a chipping bout. **I am not claiming them.**
  I did not lower the gate to manufacture a hit.

  This is an unsurprising negative: rain, morning, mic under an umbrella. The value of
  today is that **the instrument is now built and validated against a known target**, so
  when a bird does chip, we will know it is a bird and not the weather.

  off the mic. The umbrella was protecting the capsule and simultaneously acting as a *drum* —

  `20260712-091055.wav` (~t+180s). Expect handling noise as the stand is touched.

  **Everything before this is condition A (umbrella + rain). Everything after is condition B
  (open air, dry).** Do not pool them.

  **PREDICTION, written before the data exists** (informal — not a sealed hypothesis, just
  refusing to explain after the fact): the broadband-impulse count should **fall sharply**.
  Under condition A the detector rejected **136–260 weather events per 5-minute segment**.
  If those impulses are rain-on-fabric, condition B should drop that to a small fraction.
  If it does *not* drop, my rain-drum story is wrong and the impulses are coming from
  something else — which I would need to find.

  (Caveat, stated up front: the rain stopping and the umbrella coming off are **confounded** —
  they happened together, so a drop cannot tell us which one mattered. To separate them we
  would need an umbrella-on/umbrella-off comparison *in the same weather*.)

- **09:13:17 — FALSE POSITIVE, caught by inspection.** The live watcher fired: one event
  cleared the band-contrast screen in segment `091055`. It was **not a bird — it was Aaron
  walking to the rig to take the umbrella off.** The spectrogram shows enormous bursts
  blazing from DC to 22 kHz; a chip is a delicate thing living at 6–10 kHz. It slipped the
  screen (+9.2 dB) only because it was so loud and broadband.

  **A test that did NOT work, recorded because negative results count:** I hypothesized that
  high-frequency rolloff would condemn it — real chips die off above ~12 kHz, mechanical
  noise shouldn't. Measured it: reference chips sit at **−23.0 dB** (14–22 kHz vs 8–10 kHz),
  the general porch impulse population at **−7.3 dB** — a good *bulk* separator. But the
  09:13:17 event measured **−20.3 dB**, i.e. **inside the reference range.** The feature
  exonerated it. It was convicted instead by contrast (+9.2 vs +14–21), duration (18 ms vs
  32–42 ms), and context.

- **09:2x — added CONFIDENCE TIERS rather than tightening the gate.** The temptation was to
  raise the screen until the answer came out clean. That is tuning-until-it-agrees, and it
  would silently drop distant birds (a far-off chip loses high frequencies to air absorption,
  so it has *lower* contrast than a close one — the exact birds we most want to hear are the
  ones a tight gate kills). Instead the screen stays permissive and every survivor is now
  scored against the reference chips' own distribution:

  | tier | meaning |
  |---|---|
  | **STRONG** | contrast ≥ +14 dB **and** 20–60 ms — inside the real chips' range on both axes |
  | weak | right on one axis, wrong on the other |
  | doubtful | cleared the screen but looks nothing like a chip |

  Validated both directions: the reference scores **STRONG 70 / weak 24 / doubtful 1** (of 95).
  The porch today scores **STRONG 0** — every survivor weak or doubtful. The watcher now only
  shouts for STRONG; crying wolf for noise trains us to ignore the alert that matters.

- **09:20 — PREDICTION CONFIRMED.** The 09:15 segment is the first fully post-umbrella one:

  | segment | 8–10 kHz floor | broadband impulses |
  |---|---|---|
  | 08:54 | −71.1 dB | 257 |
  | 09:00 | −70.7 dB | 136 |
  | 09:05 | −69.3 dB | 208 |
  | 09:10 | −68.5 dB | 171 ← umbrella off mid-segment |
  | **09:15** | −67.5 dB | **32** |

  **171 → 32, an ~81% collapse.** The prediction (written at 09:14, before the data existed)
  was that the impulse count would fall sharply if they were rain-on-fabric, and that failure
  to fall would falsify the rain-drum story. It fell. **Supported.**

  Note the counter-intuitive part: the noise *floor* went slightly UP (−71 → −67.5 dB). With
  the umbrella gone the capsule is more exposed to ambient wind and environment, so the broad
  hiss rose ~3.5 dB — while the sharp *impulses* vanished. That is the trade we wanted: a
  slightly louder room, no drum.

  **The confound still stands** (as stated in advance): rain-stopping and umbrella-removal
  happened together. This result cannot attribute the drop to one or the other. A same-weather
  umbrella-on/off A/B is the only thing that would separate them.

- **09:35 — STORAGE. Two findings, one of which is a correction of my own claim.**

  Aaron asked for a way to trim the "not hummingbird" audio, since we'll accumulate a lot
  of it. Two experiments before designing anything.

  **(a) I was WRONG that lossy compression would hurt the science.** I asserted it without
  testing. Re-encoded the reference bird and re-ran the detector:

  | codec | chips | STRONG | interval | size |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | WAV (truth) | 95 | 70 | 0.494 s | 100% |
  | FLAC | 95 | 70 | 0.494 s | **50%** (bit-identical, hash-verified) |
  | Opus 128k | 95 | 70 | 0.494 s | ~15% |
  | **Opus 64k** | **95** | **71** | **0.494 s** | **~8%** |

  Opus 64k loses **nothing** — not a chip, not a millisecond of interval. It even nudges
  STRONG up, because the codec suppresses low-level noise and that flatters the contrast
  ratio. My "it destroys spectral detail" was a hunch, and the hunch was wrong.

  **(b) THE DETECTOR IS BLIND TO DISTANT BIRDS — and this is the real finding.**
  Took the 95 real chips, attenuated them (= a bird further away), mixed them into REAL
  porch noise from this morning, and asked the detector to find its own known ground truth:

  | chip level | chips found (of 95) | STRONG |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 dB — bird at the feeder | 78 | 31 |
  | −12 dB — bird in the next tree | **27** | **0** |
  | −20 dB | **0** | **0** |
  | −26 dB | **0** | **0** |

  Even at full volume, porch noise already costs 18% of chips and over half the STRONG
  calls (70 → 31). Twelve dB down — a bird a few metres further out — and I find barely a
  quarter of its chips and **zero** STRONG. At −20 dB I am **completely deaf.**

  **This decides the storage design.** We CANNOT delete audio because "no chips were
  detected", because the detector demonstrably fails to detect real chips that are
  genuinely there. Doing so would shred exactly the faint-bird recordings a better
  detector could someday recover — and we'd never know what we'd lost. It is the same
  error as trigger-recording, one step later in the pipeline.

  So `scripts/archive-audio.sh` **only ever compresses; it never deletes on detection.**
  Compression alone dissolves the problem anyway:

  | | per day | per year |
  |---|---|---|
  | WAV (now) | 3.7 GB | 1.3 TB ✗ |
  | FLAC lossless | 1.8 GB | 657 GB |
  | Opus 64k deep tier | **~0.3 GB** | **~106 GB** ✓ |

  Safety rails, all tested: dry-run is the default; FLAC is removed-source-only-after
  byte-identical hash verification; `--opus` **refuses** to run without `--older-than`
  (lossy must never touch fresh data); and the segment ffmpeg currently holds open is
  always excluded.

- **09:45 — THE MATCHED FILTER. The detector's range problem is fixed.**

  The energy detector asks *"is there energy in the chip band?"* — which fails on a quiet
  bird, because a quiet bird's energy simply isn't above the noise. But energy isn't all we
  know about a chip. **We know its SHAPE.** So: build a template by averaging the 95 real
  chips in the reference, slide it across the spectrogram, and ask *"how much does this
  moment look like a chip?"* (spectrogram cross-correlation — standard bioacoustics; done on
  the spectrogram rather than the waveform because two chips aren't phase-aligned, so a
  waveform matched filter would cancel itself out).

  Benchmarked against ground truth, real chips buried in real porch noise:

  | bird level | ENERGY detector | MATCHED FILTER @ 0.6 |
  |---|---|---|
  | at the feeder | 78/95 | **94/95** |
  | −12 dB (next tree) | 27/95 | **87/95** |
  | −18 dB | **1/95** (deaf) | **71/95** |
  | −24 dB | 0/95 | **46/95** |

  **~18–20 dB of hearing bought back.** `scripts/match-chips.py`.

  It also revealed my ground truth was undercounting: on clean audio the matched filter finds
  ~100 chips where the energy detector found 95, and the interval SD *stays tight* (0.094 vs
  0.085). Noise would have shattered that regularity. Those extras are real chips the energy
  detector missed.

- **09:50 — TWO FALSE-BIRD TRAPS, both caught, both instructive.**

  The matched filter's cost is false alarms (4–8/min). Two layers kill them, and neither is
  a better filter — both are **rhythm**:

  **(1) Rain has no rhythm.** A chipping bird is a bout; rain is Poisson. Requiring ≥4
  detections in a row at plausible chip spacing (0.15–0.8 s) drops porch noise to nothing:
  41 raw detections in a rainy segment → **zero bouts**.

  **(2) But FOOTSTEPS DO have a rhythm — and it fooled me.** The first bout-enabled scan
  triumphantly reported a hummingbird at **09:13:14**. It was **Aaron walking to the rig**.
  Human walking cadence is ~0.4–0.5 s per step, which sits *squarely inside* the chip-interval
  window. A spacing test alone cannot tell a hummingbird from a person.

  What separates them is **regularity**. A chipping bird is a metronome; a walker is not:

  | | CV (SD/mean of intervals) |
  |---|---|
  | real bird, clean | 0.15, 0.20 |
  | real bird, −18 dB in noise | 0.07, 0.09, 0.16, 0.20 |
  | **Aaron's footsteps** | **0.60** — 3× more ragged than any real bout |

  Added `BOUT_MAX_CV = 0.35`. Regression, all four green: real bird ✓ detected, distant bird
  (−18 dB) ✓ still detected, footsteps ✓ rejected, whole morning ✓ clear.

- **09:55 — RE-ANALYZED THE ENTIRE MORNING with the new detector.** This is the dividend of
  recording continuously instead of on a trigger: *same audio, better ears.* If a bird had
  chipped across the yard at 09:00, the old detector was deaf to it — but the audio was still
  on disk, so the new one could go find it.

  **Result: still zero hummingbirds, 08:54–09:45.** Which agrees with Aaron's ears. A null
  confirmed twice, by two independent methods, is a real result.

### ⚠️ THE BAND-CONTRAST GATE IS DEAD. Killed by our own bird, hours after I built it.

This morning's proudest result — band contrast separating bird (+17.8 dB) from rain
(−14.7 dB) by 32 dB with zero overlap — **only worked because the reference was a pristine,
close-miked recordist's bird.** Measured against a REAL bird in a REAL yard:

|  | p10 | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| reference (close-up) | +22.7 | +28.7 | +33.1 |
| **OUR REAL BIRD** | **+1.6** | **+5.9** | **+8.9** |
| rain | −15.3 | −8.4 | −0.1 |
| **Aaron's footsteps** | −13.9 | −5.2 | **+4.5** |

**ALL REAL BIRDS p10 = +1.1 dB. ALL NOISE p90 = +1.8 dB. The populations OVERLAP.**

A real bird at real distance loses its high frequencies to air and foliage. **There is no
threshold that keeps our hummingbird and rejects our footsteps.** Had I trusted that gate,
it would have thrown away a confirmed hummingbird as noise. The gate is demoted to a cheap
pre-screen; its STRONG/weak/doubtful tiers are known-wrong and must not be read as truth.

**WHAT ACTUALLY SURVIVED CONTACT WITH A REAL BIRD:**

1. **Matched filter** (chip *shape*, not energy) — +18 dB of range
2. **Bout + regularity (CV ≤ 0.35)** — ***the*** discriminator. A bird is a metronome; rain
   is a Poisson process; footsteps are a stumble. It found our bird and rejected BOTH.
3. **Coincidence across the 3-mic array** — 6.9σ. Only a real sound in the world does that.

### Two parameters inherited from the paper were WRONG for our birds

1. **The band.** The paper's detector used 8–10 kHz; I treated it as fact. But that was the
   window *they* chose for *their* recordings. Both birds actually peak at **6–7 kHz**:

   | band | our bird | reference |
   |---|---|---|
   | 8–10 kHz (paper's) | **−1.5 dB** | +17.9 dB |
   | **5–10 kHz** (retuned) | **+5.9 dB** | **+28.7 dB** |

   **+7.5 dB for free**, and rain false-positives fell **405 → 6**.

2. **The template.** Rebuilt from **62 confirmed chips off Aaron's own porch**. It finds
   **2× the chips** on our birds, still finds the reference, still rejects rain. The two
   templates are 0.77 similar — same call, different air.

**The general lesson, learned twice in one day: a parameter validated on someone else's clean
recording is a HYPOTHESIS about your data, not a fact about it. Our birds are the ground
truth now.**

### The seed of a real hypothesis (NOT a claim)

Our bird's intervals cluster at **~0.25 s** — *faster* than the published male mean (0.32 s),
far from female (0.43 s). **This is NOT a sex determination and must not be reported as one:**
the published distributions overlap heavily, chip rate varies with behaviour (the paper found
territorial chipping is faster than chasing), and this is n=1 bird on one morning against
their 106 recordings. It is, however, exactly the kind of thing that should be **sealed as a
hypothesis BEFORE the data that tests it exists.** See `science/README.md`.

## THE RIG, REBUILT (afternoon of 2026-07-12) — three mics, zero dollars

Aaron floated a 20 ft boom with a parabolic dish aimed at the nectar tree. The dish physics
is genuinely excellent — gain scales as (D/λ)², so a 20" dish boosts 9 kHz chips by ~+27 dB
while giving **zero** gain to wind rumble (at 200 Hz it's a fraction of a wavelength and the
sound simply diffracts around it). It is a hardware implementation of the band-contrast gate.

**But he already owned something better: two Rode Wireless GO transmitters with lav capsules.**

- The GO's built-in mic is **omnidirectional** (Rode datasheet: 50 Hz–20 kHz) — the exact
  capsule a dish wants. But they're **wireless**, which makes the boom unnecessary: you can
  put the mic *at the flowers* instead of building a 20 ft lever to reach toward them.
- **Inverse square beats the dish.** 15 ft → 1 ft is **+23 dB**, for the price of tape.
- **The lav attachment is what makes it survivable.** Capsule at the flowers on a thin
  cable; TX body sheltered and dry on the trunk. Only a cheap, replaceable capsule is
  exposed to PNW weather — not the $200 transmitter.

**The rig now:**

| mic | position | role |
|---|---|---|
| `shotgun` | porch (M-Track Duo ch1, +48V) | wide shot **and control** — if a close mic hears a chip and this doesn't, the close mic is earning its keep |
| `tx1` | nectar tree, at the flowers | the money mic |
| `tx2` | the new feeder | catches the moment they *discover* it |

**Two traps found and closed while wiring it up:**

1. **DEVICE INDICES DRIFT.** Plugging in the Rode moved the M-Track from `[3]` to `[4]`.
   A hardcoded index would have silently recorded **the wrong microphone** — the worst kind
   of failure, because the data looks perfectly fine. `record-loop.sh` now resolves every
   device **by name**, every launch.

2. **THE RODE WAS IN MERGED MODE.** Both transmitters were being mixed into one signal and
   copied to both channels (verified: ch1 − ch2 = −inf dB, bit-identical). The tree mic and
   the feeder mic would have been one indistinguishable blob. Fixed by holding both RX
   buttons 3 s (split = two meters on screen, merged = one). **`record-loop.sh` now probes
   for this and REFUSES to record merged** — this trap is closed permanently.

   Verified after the fix: tx1 −64.9 dB, tx2 −63.9 dB, difference −61.4 dB → genuinely two
   separate mics.

**Levels to re-check before the dawn session:** the shotgun currently runs ~29 dB hotter than
the transmitters. Partly environment (shotgun outdoors, TXs on a table indoors), partly gain.
**Once the lavs are at the flowers, re-measure** — a chip at one inch may be loud enough to
clip.
